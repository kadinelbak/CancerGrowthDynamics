Neural vs mechanistic — where each should be used

How to read this

The neural models (MLP, UDE with a neural kill term, pure neural ODE) are a competing method, not a step towards the mechanistic model. Each row takes the best neural and the best mechanistic model on one test. Errors are forward-prediction RMSE on the log scale (0.2 ≈ 22% typical error); margin is how much the neural model is better (positive) or worse (negative). Differences under 0.01 count as a tie. The closed form counts as mechanistic even though its terms were suggested by the neural kill term.

Scorecard

On held-out tests (data the model never saw): neural wins 2, mechanistic wins 2, ties 2. Rows marked in-sample fit only show flexibility — a more flexible model always fits its own training data better — and BIC weighs fit against the number of parameters.

Where the neural model is the better choice

  • Forward prediction — held out: ce1 treated co-culture
  • Forward prediction — train: untreated co-culture (in-sample fit)
  • Forward prediction — train: drug units (in-sample fit)
  • Hold-out: hold out off→on intermittent

Interpretation. The neural kill term (UDE) is most useful where the drug response has a shape the Hill curve or a single exposure filter cannot express — for example a delayed onset when the drug is added late (the off→on hold-out) and the density dependence of the kill in co-culture. In those settings it predicts held-out wells as well as or slightly better than the mechanistic models. It was also useful as a discovery tool: its learned kill surface suggested the density-dependent terms (v·ρ, v²·ρ) of the closed form, which then beat the Hill model on BIC.

Where the mechanistic model is the better choice

  • Hold-out: hold out on→off intermittent
  • Hold-out: hold out 1 uM monoculture
  • BIC on training drug units (lower = better fit per parameter) (fit per parameter)

Interpretation. Mechanistic models win when the model must extrapolate: an unseen dose, an unseen seeding density, or an unseen schedule where the drug is given first. With 12–28 training wells, 4 dose levels and 2 switching schedules, the ~110 neural weights are weakly constrained outside the training conditions (the pure neural ODE fails on the unseen 1 uM dose), and BIC penalises them heavily. Mechanistic parameters (growth rate, carrying capacity, EC50-like terms) also have biological meaning and bootstrap intervals, which the neural weights do not.

Recommendation by task

Short-term (next-day) forecasting: any of the ODE models or boosted trees; neural adds nothing measurable.
Predicting held-out wells under tested conditions (same doses/schedules): the UDE is competitive and sometimes best — a reasonable choice, ideally as an ensemble alongside the closed form.
Optimal control (new schedules and doses): use the mechanistic closed form as the primary model, and the UDE as a cross-check — where they disagree (e.g. A2780 monoculture schedules), treat the prediction as uncertain.
Understanding mechanism / reporting parameters: mechanistic.
When the neural model would start to pay off: many more conditions (doses, schedules, densities, time-varying dosing), or inputs with no obvious mechanistic form (e.g. image features).
