Simplifying A2780 Coculture Adaptive Therapy

A two-population mathematical core, a focused ovarian-cancer reading set, and an experimental roadmap for explaining how untreated competition, cisplatin exposure, and adaptive treatment decisions fit together.

Interpretive boundary: this is a project-relevance ranking, not a claim that these are the 15 most influential ovarian-cancer papers in general. A paper can justify testing a model class, but only this project's data and prospective validation can support selecting it for A2780.

Start Here: The Smallest Defensible Model

The current experiment directly observes two established cell lines: A2780Naive, written \(N(t)\), and A2780cis, written \(C(t)\). The clearest adaptive-therapy model should begin with exactly those two populations. A2780cis may be less responsive to cisplatin, but it is not automatically a hidden mixture of sensitive and tolerant cells, and treatment does not convert A2780Naive into the A2780cis cell line.

1Grow separatelyLearn the untreated growth law for each lineage from monoculture.
2Compete togetherAdd only the cross-lineage effects needed to explain untreated coculture.
3Apply exposureLet the same fitted coculture state continue while cisplatin exposure rises and clears.
4Decide at checksAt monitoring times, choose the next allowed dose from the measured response.

Step 1: Untreated coculture

\[L_N=N+\alpha_{NC}C,\qquad L_C=C+\alpha_{CN}N\] \[\frac{dN}{dt}=G_N(N,L_N)-d_NN,\qquad \frac{dC}{dt}=G_C(C,L_C)-d_CC\]

\(G_N\) and \(G_C\) are the already fitted logistic or theta-logistic growth laws. \(L_N\) and \(L_C\) are effective crowding loads. If \(\alpha_{NC}=1\), one cis cell contributes the same modeled load to Naive as one Naive cell. If \(\alpha_{NC}>1\), cis has a stronger suppressive effect on Naive. The reverse effect is controlled independently by \(\alpha_{CN}\). The loss terms \(d_N\) and \(d_C\) are included only when the data support lineage-specific decline beyond inherited growth.

Step 2: Cisplatin exposure and kill

\[\frac{dE}{dt}=k_{in}D_{cmd}(t)-k_{clear}E\] \[\frac{dN}{dt}=G_N(N,L_N)-d_NN-q_N(E)N,\qquad \frac{dC}{dt}=G_C(C,L_C)-d_CC-q_C(E)C\] \[q_i(E)=E_{max,i}\frac{E^h}{EC_{50,i}^{h}+E^h}\]

\(D_{cmd}(t)\) is what the experimenter adds; \(E(t)\) is effective exposure; and \(q_i(E)\) is the lineage-specific loss pressure. These are not the same quantity. The one-state exposure equation lets drug action decay after washout without creating an unexplained permanent effect. A second damage compartment should be added only if this model fails held-out pulse and washout experiments.

Step 3: Adaptive decision rule

\[ D_{k+1}=\begin{cases} D_{high}, & B(t_k)\ge U,\\ 0, & B(t_k)\le L,\\ D_k, & L<B(t_k)<U, \end{cases} \qquad B(t)=N(t)+C(t) \]

The model is integrated continuously, but treatment changes only at prespecified monitoring times \(t_k\). \(U\) and \(L\) are upper and lower burden thresholds. The middle rule is hysteresis: between the thresholds, the previous treatment state is retained so noise does not cause rapid on/off switching. Dose modulation can replace \(0\) and \(D_{high}\) with a small set of allowed doses.

Measured states\(N\), \(C\), and total \(B=N+C\). These are appropriate default graph outputs.
Fitted biologyGrowth parameters, \(\alpha_{NC}\), \(\alpha_{CN}\), supported loss terms, and dose-response parameters.
Experimental exposureCommanded concentration, refresh and washout times, and fitted effective clearance.
Policy choicesMonitoring times, thresholds, and allowable doses. These are schedule settings, not biological fit parameters.

When adaptive therapy can work

All four conditions should be checked rather than assumed:

  1. Differential drug response: cisplatin suppresses A2780Naive more strongly than A2780cis over the tested range.
  2. Off-drug recovery advantage: without drug, Naive regrows at least fast enough to recover ecological pressure on cis.
  3. Meaningful competition: the two populations limit one another at the densities used; at very low density, preserving sensitive cells may provide little suppression.
  4. Observable feedback: the quantity controlling treatment can be measured often enough and with known error.
Recommended default: use \(N\), \(C\), one effective-exposure state, and discrete dose decisions. Keep phenotype switching, hidden cis compartments, molecular PK, and spatial structure under Advanced Models until new measurements identify them.

What The Literature Changes Mathematically

The papers support a sequence of increasingly rich models. Richness should be earned by new measurements, not by a lower training error alone.

1. Observable two-lineage competition should remain the default

For the present experiment, A2780Naive and total A2780cis are measured. The most defensible default therefore keeps those two observables explicit:

\[\frac{dN}{dt}=G_N(N,L_N)-d_NN-E_N(t,D,L_N)N,\qquad \frac{dC}{dt}=G_C(C,L_C)-d_CC-E_C(t,D,L_C)C\]

with effective loads \(L_N=N+\alpha_{NC}C\) and \(L_C=C+\alpha_{CN}N\). Hockings and Valdivia support testing asymmetric fitness and lineage-specific loss, but neither paper proves that the A2780cis observations contain separately identifiable sensitive-like and tolerant-like states.

Decision: keep latent cis states out of the default graph and default scientific interpretation. Add them only when a marker, sorting assay, or perturbation distinguishes the states.
2. Drug command, extracellular exposure, damage, and cell loss are different quantities

El-Kareh and Secomb support an exposure-to-damage chain rather than a single drug-on flag:

\[\frac{dc_i}{dt}=k_{in}D_{ext}(t)-k_{out}c_i-k_bc_i,\qquad \frac{dc_b}{dt}=k_bc_i-k_{repair}c_b,\qquad E(t)=E_{max}\frac{c_b^h}{EC_{50}^h+c_b^h}\]

This abstraction separates media concentration \(D_{ext}\), intracellular free platinum \(c_i\), bound or damage-associated platinum \(c_b\), and the downstream loss rate \(E\). The full published model contains parameter combinations that cannot be separated from survival data alone, so the project should not fit every rate to total cell counts.

Decision: fit a minimal external-exposure plus one damage-state model first. Add a second intracellular state only if uptake or DNA-bound platinum is measured.
3. Resistance cost, competition, and turnover must be disentangled experimentally

A low-parameter benchmark consistent with Strobl's analysis is:

\[\dot S=r_S S\left(1-\frac{S+R}{K}\right)-d_TS-u(t)k_SS,\qquad \dot R=r_R R\left(1-\frac{S+R}{K}\right)-d_TR-u(t)k_RR\]

A smaller \(r_R\) is a growth cost; a larger resistant death term is a survival cost; and competition becomes strongest near carrying capacity. Similar trajectories can arise from different combinations of these terms. Hockings directly measured proliferation and apoptosis, showing the kind of additional data needed to separate them.

Decision: do not infer a resistance cost from declining resistant abundance alone. Measure EdU/Ki-67 and apoptosis/death alongside lineage counts under high- and low-resource conditions.
4. Patient translation requires a new observation model

Jitmana et al. treat CA-125 as a longitudinal proxy for burden while separating on-treatment and off-treatment rates. A future clinical model needs an explicit link between hidden tumor populations and the measured biomarker:

\[S'(t)=\gamma_SS-\delta_Su(t)S-\mu S,\qquad R'(t)=\gamma_RR-\delta_Ru(t)R+\mu S,\qquad y_{CA125}(t)=h(S+R)+\varepsilon(t)\]

The observation function \(h\), biomarker delay, surgery, lesion detection, and patient-level variability are new model components. They cannot be created by rescaling plate carrying capacity.

Decision: maintain a strict separation between the laboratory simulator and any future clinical simulator.

Future Research Roadmap

NOW: 0-6 months

Make the current mechanism testable

  1. Keep the observable \(N,C\) model as the default and move latent cis splits to advanced diagnostics.
  2. Add a minimal exposure/damage candidate with explicit media refresh and washout.
  3. Refit simple competition, growth-cost, death-cost, and turnover candidates to the identical objective.
  4. Use sample-aware hierarchical fits and propagate Stage 1-3 uncertainty into Stage 4.
  5. Pre-register held-out schedules and directional predictions before collecting more data.
NEXT: 6-18 months

Collect identifying measurements

  1. Repeat coculture across starting ratios and densities with lineage-resolved counts.
  2. Measure proliferation and apoptosis simultaneously.
  3. Compare direct contact, transwell, and conditioned media.
  4. Run pulse-washout experiments with multiple exposure durations and refresh intervals.
  5. Prospectively test at least two model-discriminating adaptive schedules.
LATER: 18+ months

Build the translational bridge

  1. Move to spheroids or organoids before adding spatial drug penetration.
  2. Calibrate a separate human-scale growth and observation model to CA-125, imaging, or cfDNA.
  3. Add surgery, lesion emergence, toxicity, and interpatient variability.
  4. Run virtual trials only after external validation.
  5. Keep clinical dose recommendations outside the scope of the research simulator.
Models not ready for default use: reversible phenotype switching, multi-mechanism resistance branching, intracellular molecular networks, spatial penetration, and patient survival models are scientifically worthwhile. They are not identifiable from the current two-color cell-count trajectories alone.

Analysis requirements

  • Define training and held-out conditions before fitting.
  • Average technical wells within a biological sample; retain sample identity.
  • Fit all candidate models to the same observations and scaling rule.
  • Report raw and trajectory-scaled residuals.
  • Count every fitted parameter correctly in BIC.
  • Reject nonfinite fits, solver failures, and failure sentinels.
  • Use multistart stability and two-sided profile bounds.
  • Bootstrap predictions and schedules, not just parameter tables.
  • Test predictive coverage on unseen schedules.
  • Record which conclusions survive model uncertainty.
