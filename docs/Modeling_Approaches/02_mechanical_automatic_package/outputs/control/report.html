Optimal control of cisplatin dosing

Generated 2026-10-07 23:09 by run_control.jl and control_report.jl. Sequential model-predictive control (MPC) chooses the cisplatin concentration for every 2-day interval between media changes, using the carried compartment models: normal medium (Stage 3a) and low resource (Stage 4), starting from the nominal 25:75, 50:50 and 75:25 seedings (100 cells split by the ratio). Two drug-response scenarios are run throughout: A the coculture fit as selected (Stage 3a) and B the monoculture drug response for both lines. The control goal is a low total tumour burden with resistant cells held back (a resistant cell counts as two), with a literature-based toxicity barrier and a soft burden penalty (a relaxed barrier above B_cancer, not a hard cap); MPC minimises this weighted cost, not the burden alone. Every decision looks 14 days ahead, also the last ones, so each schedule is the first 7 decisions of a continuing policy. Everything the controller adds to the fitted biology is listed with its source below. Every result is a model simulation: none of these schedules has been tested experimentally yet. A 120-day version runs the same controller past the end of the data, with fasting (low resource) as a second control.

The model the controller uses

The cell equations are exactly the carried compartment models, with the same parameter values: the control plant simulates a flattened, type-stable copy of the fits' right-hand side (tests check it against the zoo's own right-hand side to 10⁻¹², and that the plant reproduces the fitted Stage 3a curves at 1 µM to 10⁻⁵). Every run starts from the nominal seeding (100 cells split by the ratio, e.g. 25:75 → N = 25, C = 75), not from a measured day-0 count. The controller only adds the dose input \(u\) and a toxicity proxy. Drug → effect → kill follows \(u \to A_{X,1} \to A_{X,2} \to A_{X,3}=E_X \to\) kill, and separately \(u \to B \to T\).

Objective, soft penalties and barriers

\[\min_{u_1,\dots,u_H}\ \sum_{k=1}^{H}\ell(x_{k+1},u_k)+\phi(x_{H+1}),\qquad 0\le u_k\le u_{max}\]
\[\ell=q_B\,\tfrac{N+C}{K}+q_R\,\tfrac{C}{K}+q_T\Big(\tfrac{T}{B_{tox}}\Big)^2+r_u\Big(\tfrac{u}{u_{max}}\Big)^2+b_{\mu_C}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{N+C}{B_{cancer}}\Big)+b_{\mu_T}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{T}{B_{tox}}\Big)\]
\[b_\mu(z)=\begin{cases}-\mu\log z, & z\ge\delta\\ \mu\left[\tfrac12\left(\tfrac{z-2\delta}{\delta}\right)^2-\tfrac12-\log\delta\right], & z<\delta\end{cases}\qquad \phi=5.0\left[q_B\,\tfrac{N+C}{K}+q_R\,\tfrac{C}{K}+q_T\Big(\tfrac{T}{B_{tox}}\Big)^2\right]\]

Goal. Keep the total tumour burden low and hold resistant cells back. The burden term \(q_B(N+C)/K\) is linear: every cell costs the same however large the tumour is, so the controller still treats a small culture when the drug removes cells. The resistance premium \(q_R\,C/K\) makes a resistant cell cost more than a sensitive one (with \(q_R=q_B\) it counts as two cells), because further cisplatin cannot remove it. Sensitive cells are never rewarded: a schedule that leaves more cells in total is worse unless it buys a larger drop in resistant cells. On top of the linear burden there is a soft burden penalty (a relaxed barrier above B_cancer = 0.8 K), not a hard cap. MPC therefore minimises this weighted cost (burden, resistance premium, toxicity, dose and the soft penalties), not the burden alone, so a fixed policy can end with fewer cells; each results section compares MPC with the best fixed policy on every metric and plots all policies on final cells against dose. Precedents: tumour burden as a linear cost (Schättler & Ledzewicz 2015) and in-vitro chemotherapy optimised to avoid resistant growth (Carrère 2017).

States are normalised (by K and B_tox) so that numbers of cells do not swamp the toxicity terms. The burden penalty grows as N + C nears B_cancer and favours treating; the toxicity barrier grows as T nears B_tox and favours stopping; the schedule is the balance of the two, not a hard-coded rule. The relaxed barrier (Feller & Ebenbauer 2017) is the exact log barrier inside the limit and a steep quadratic below z = δ, so a state beyond the limit still gives a finite, informative cost; exceedances are counted in the tables (whether and for how many days), never hidden. Defaults: q_B = q_R = q_T = 1, r_u = 0.1, μ = 0.05, δ = 0.02, terminal factor 5.0; the weights (including q_R = 0, burden only, and q_R = 3) are swept below.

Why a penalty and not a hard cap. In normal medium the culture grows towards its capacity K under the measured doses: 13 of 42 normal-medium runs (3 ratios × 2 scenarios × 7 policies) pass B_cancer = 0.8 K at some time, the data-supported MPC in 1 of 6 (for 0.0 days, median). As a hard state constraint, every schedule that cannot hold the culture below B_cancer would be infeasible and the optimiser would have no cost to rank them by; as a penalty, they are still ranked, and the exceedance is reported. A relaxed log barrier is, by construction, a soft-constraint (penalty) formulation (Feller & Ebenbauer 2017), and in cancer optimal control a tumour-burden limit is usually a penalised state constraint or a progression threshold rather than a hard bound (e.g. adaptive-therapy thresholds set relative to the burden, Zhang et al. 2017). B_cancer is therefore reported as the level where a soft penalty starts to dominate. Controller: sequential (SQP) MPC. At each decision time: (1) simulate the nonlinear model over the horizon with the current plan; (2) linearise F along it (A_k, B_k by automatic differentiation); (3) gradient by the adjoint recursion \(\mu_k=\nabla\ell_k+A_k^\top\mu_{k+1}\) (vector–Jacobian products); (4) Gauss–Newton Hessian from the forward sensitivities \(S_{k+1}=A_kS_k+B_ke_k^\top\); (5) solve the box-constrained quadratic subproblem; (6) line search on the true nonlinear cost; repeat to convergence; apply the first dose; re-plan at the next media change. The local subproblem has the LQR structure \(\delta x_{k+1}=A_k\delta x_k+B_k\delta u_k\), but it is re-linearised every iteration, bounded, and has non-quadratic barriers, so this is successive-linearisation MPC, not a fixed LQR.

Two modes. Data-supported: doses restricted to 0, 0.62, 1.0 and 1.47 µM (the measured doses); the finite-horizon problem is then solved exactly by searching every dose sequence (prefix sharing, a warm-start bound and pruning), 4⁷ sequences over the 14-day look-ahead at each of the 7 media changes. Demonstration: continuous doses up to 3.0 µM with SQP; above 1.47 µM the linear kill is extrapolated and the result is not supported by data.

Fixed look-ahead. Every decision, including the last ones, plans 14 days ahead; near the end of the run the plan simply runs past day 14 (the model is simulated beyond it). The schedule shown is therefore the first 7 decisions of a continuing policy, read off at day 14, not a plan that stops caring about what happens after its last day (a shrinking horizon stops dosing near the end, because later regrowth no longer counts).

Robustness over bootstrap parameter sets

The schedules above are planned with the best-fit parameters. Here each one is replayed on bootstrap parameter sets: the growth, drug and coculture parameters of the same bootstrap replicate of their own stages (an approximation: each stage was bootstrapped with earlier-stage values held at their estimates). Scenario B's crowding was fitted to the untreated cocultures, which have no bootstrap of their own: each draw borrows the Stage 3a bootstrap ratio (replicate ÷ estimate) of the same parameter and applies it to the scenario-B value, so its bands are an approximation too.

Closed loop. MPC plans with the best-fit model and runs on the bootstrap plant. It sees only the cell counts N and C at each media change, with 10% multiplicative measurement noise (log-normal, mean 1; an assumption, control_config.toml [observation]), and reconstructs every hidden state (drug-effect chains, systemic burden B, toxicity T) by simulating its own model under the doses it has applied. Threshold adaptive therapy re-decides on each plant from the same noisy counts. The comparison is paired: on the first 40 draws every policy meets the same plant and the same noise, and the second table compares the closed loop with each policy draw by draw on exactly those draws.

  • The control plant at 1 µM reproduces the fitted Stage 3a coculture model (scenario A) and the held-out construction of scenario B (Ce0 crowding with the Stage 2b kills) on the 50:50 treated coculture (relative difference < 10⁻⁵); the flattened right-hand side equals the zoo's own to 10⁻¹².
  • Fixed-step RK4 F(x, u) matches an adaptive Tsit5 solve (< 10⁻⁵ relative; the Norton–Simon factor has a kink at capacity, where RK4 loses its smooth-case order) at random biologically valid states.
  • Automatic-differentiation Jacobians A, B match central finite differences (< 10⁻⁴ relative, as the differences straddle the same kink); finite differences are used only here, never in the optimiser.
  • Linearisation error falls 4× when the perturbation halves (second order).
  • The adjoint (vector–Jacobian) gradient of the cost matches finite differences (< 10⁻⁴ relative).
  • Every stage and terminal cost is ≥ 0 at random states (what makes pruning in the exact search safe), and the cost split by term adds up to the cost.
  • The exact discrete search equals brute force; SQP never breaks the dose bounds and never increases the cost; at the SQP solution the projected gradient is near zero, and SQP over [0, 1.47] µM is not worse than the exact optimum over the four measured doses (a subset).
  • The nominal closed loop with the fixed look-ahead equals replaying its own schedule, and each of its decisions equals the first move of the exact plan from the replayed state.

Tests of two model assumptions

Drug washout. The drug-effect compartments assume the effect decays after the drug is removed at the same rate it built up. The intermittent experiments (1 µM on then off, or off then on, at day 7) test this: a separate washout rate is not supported (A2780: ΔBIC +2.46; A2780cis: ΔBIC +3.10; a negative value beyond −3 would be needed). The equal-rate model is kept, and its no-refit predictions of those experiments are on the Validation page.

What the data do not identify

  • The toxicity limit is inactive at the default settings. Since \(dT/dt=(1-T)\big[a_H(1-e^{-B})-r_H\,T\big]\), T can never exceed \(a_H/r_H\) = 0.1, below the bound B_tox = 0.25, whatever the dose. The toxicity term in the cost still discourages dosing, but the toxicity barrier only comes close to binding in the sweep with slower host recovery (r_H = 0.2 /day, bound \(a_H/r_H\) = 0.5). The schedules are therefore not toxicity-constrained in the main runs.
  • How each scenario is validated. Scenario A (the Stage 3a coculture fit) passes leave-one-ratio-out validation, but it was fitted to the treated cocultures, so there is no held-out test of its drug response in coculture. Scenario B is the construction that was tested on held-out data: crowding fitted to the untreated cocultures only, then the treated cocultures predicted with no refit (Validation).
  • Dose response outside 0.62–1.47 µM. The monocultures were treated at three doses and the cocultures at 1 µM only. The carried kill is linear in the drug effect, so it never saturates: it cannot show a maximum kill at high doses. Continuous-dose control above 1.47 µM (demonstration mode) extrapolates the linear kill.
  • Resistant kill in coculture. The cocultures were treated at one dose only, so how strongly cisplatin kills A2780cis in coculture rests on the monoculture fit: scenario A uses the coculture fit, which selected no cisplatin term for A2780cis in coculture (β_C = 0; the monoculture data need one, +59 BIC without it) and scenario B the monoculture drug response for both lines; every result is given for both, and the two bracket the resistant kill in coculture.
  • Toxicity. Nothing in these in-vitro data measures host toxicity. Its structure and values come from de Pillis & Radunskaya (2001); the mapping from medium concentration to systemic burden (s_B) and B_cancer are assumptions. Treat toxicity-limited results as illustrative.
  • Beyond 14 days. The 28-day runs extrapolate every stage beyond the data.
  • Low resource + drug. Stage 4 is fitted to a single 1:1 culture at one dose; its low-resource strengths ε_N, ε_C are identified only for that condition. Its first observation is one day after exposure started; the hidden drug and stress states at that point are simulated once from the candidates' starting values and held fixed during fitting (a small approximation).
  • Parameter uncertainty propagation. The bootstrap combines replicates across stages, but each stage was bootstrapped with earlier-stage values held fixed, so the bands are approximate. Scenario B's crowding (fitted to the untreated cocultures) has no bootstrap of its own: its draws borrow the Stage 3a bootstrap ratios (replicate ÷ estimate) of the same parameters.
  • Measurement and state estimation. The closed-loop runs assume each media change gives a count of N and C with 10% noise and that the hidden drug-effect and toxicity states follow the planner's own model; a real loop needs the two lines counted separately at each media change, as the coculture experiments did.

Experiments that would most improve control

  1. Coculture dose series (e.g. 0.25, 0.5, 2 and 4 µM at 50:50, 14 days): identifies the coculture dose response and whether A2780cis is killed in coculture (scenario A vs B), the largest structural uncertainty for control, and lets the shape of the dose response (linear or saturating) be estimated in the condition that is being controlled.
  2. Coculture washout / intermittent schedules (1 µM on for 2 or 4 days then off, repeated): tests the drug-effect decay and the regrowth after removal in the controlled system; the monoculture intermittent data only test it for single lines.
  3. Replicate-level counts over time. The sensitivity analysis ranks sensitive growth r_N, coculture death d_N and the crowding coefficients as the main drivers of the controlled outcome; more replicates of the untreated and treated cocultures narrow exactly those.
  4. Low resource + cisplatin at a second dose. Stage 4 has one condition; a second dose separates the low-resource strengths from a drug × low-resource interaction.
  5. A toxicity readout (e.g. viability of a non-cancer line such as fibroblasts under the same schedules) would replace the literature toxicity proxy.
