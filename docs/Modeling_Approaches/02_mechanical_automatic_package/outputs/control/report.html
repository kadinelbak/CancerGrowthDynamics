Optimal control of cisplatin dosing

Generated 2026-10-06 16:16 by run_control.jl and control_report.jl. Sequential model-predictive control (MPC) chooses the cisplatin concentration for every 2-day interval between media changes, using the carried compartment models: normal medium (Stage 3a) and low resource (Stage 4), starting from the 25:75, 50:50 and 75:25 seedings. Two drug-response scenarios are run throughout: A the coculture fit as selected (Stage 3a) and B the monoculture drug response for both lines. The control goal is a low total tumour burden with resistant cells held back (a resistant cell counts as two), within a literature-based toxicity limit and a hard cap on the burden; everything it adds to the fitted biology is listed with its source below. Every result is a model simulation: none of these schedules has been tested experimentally yet. A 30-day version runs the same controller past the end of the data.

The model the controller uses

The cell equations are exactly the carried compartment models: the control plant calls the same right-hand side as the fits, with the same parameter values (a test checks that it reproduces the fitted Stage 3a curves at 1 µM to 10⁻⁵). The controller only adds the dose input \(u\) and a toxicity proxy. Drug → effect → kill follows \(u \to A_{X,1} \to A_{X,2} \to A_{X,3}=E_X \to\) kill, and separately \(u \to B \to T\).

\[\frac{dN}{dt}=r_{N}\,N\left(1-\frac{N+\alpha_{NC}C}{K}\right)-\left(1-\frac{N+\alpha_{NC}C}{K}\right)\left[\beta_{N}\,A_{N,3}\,N\right]\]
\[\frac{dA_{N,1}}{dt}=k_{N}\left(D-A_{N,1}\right),\ \frac{dA_{N,j}}{dt}=k_{N}\left(A_{N,j-1}-A_{N,j}\right),\ j=2,\dots,3\]
\[\frac{dC}{dt}=r_{C}\,C\left[1-\left(\frac{C+\alpha_{CN}N}{K}\right)^{\theta_{C}}\right]-\left[1-\left(\frac{C+\alpha_{CN}N}{K}\right)^{\theta_{C}}\right]\left[\beta_{C}\,A_{C,3}\,C\right]\]
\[\frac{dA_{C,1}}{dt}=k_{C}\left(D-A_{C,1}\right),\ \frac{dA_{C,j}}{dt}=k_{C}\left(A_{C,j-1}-A_{C,j}\right),\ j=2,\dots,3\]
\[\frac{dB}{dt}=s_B\,u-d_B\,B,\qquad \frac{dT}{dt}=a_H\left(1-e^{-B}\right)(1-T)-r_H\,T(1-T)\]

Low resource (D = u, uR = 0.9547738693467337). The low-resource strengths depend on the dose, \(\varepsilon_X(u)=1+(\varepsilon_X^{S4}-1)\min(u/1\,\mu\mathrm{M},1)\): with no drug the model is the low-resource fit alone (\(\varepsilon=1\)), and at 1 µM it is exactly the Stage 4 fit. This is the same plant as the 30-day fasting controller held in low resource throughout, so the two reports agree.

\[\frac{dN}{dt}=r_{N}\,N\left(1-\frac{N+\alpha_{NC}C}{\frac{K}{1+c_{N}\varepsilon_{N}\,u_R}}\right)-\left(1-\frac{N+\alpha_{NC}C}{\frac{K}{1+c_{N}\varepsilon_{N}\,u_R}}\right)\left[\beta_{N}\,A_{N,3}\,N\right]\]
\[\frac{dA_{N,1}}{dt}=k_{N}\left(D-A_{N,1}\right),\ \frac{dA_{N,j}}{dt}=k_{N}\left(A_{N,j-1}-A_{N,j}\right),\ j=2,\dots,3\]
\[\frac{dC}{dt}=r_{C}\,C\left[1-\left(\frac{C+\alpha_{CN}N}{K}\right)^{\theta_{C}}\right]-\left[1-\left(\frac{C+\alpha_{CN}N}{K}\right)^{\theta_{C}}\right]\left[\beta_{C}\,A_{C,3}\,C\right]-\left(d_{C}^{LR}\varepsilon_{C}\,u_R+d_{C}^{acc}S_{C,3}\right)C\]
\[\frac{dA_{C,1}}{dt}=k_{C}\left(D-A_{C,1}\right),\ \frac{dA_{C,j}}{dt}=k_{C}\left(A_{C,j-1}-A_{C,j}\right),\ j=2,\dots,3\]
\[\frac{dS_{C,1}}{dt}=k_{C}^{R}\left(\varepsilon_{C}\,u_R-S_{C,1}\right),\ \frac{dS_{C,j}}{dt}=k_{C}^{R}\left(S_{C,j-1}-S_{C,j}\right),\ j=2,\dots,3\]

Discrete transition over one decision interval (Δ = 2.0 days, u constant): \(x_{k+1}=F(x_k,u_k)\), fixed-step RK4 with step 0.05 day. Local linearisation \(A_k=\partial F/\partial x\), \(B_k=\partial F/\partial u\) by forward-mode automatic differentiation through the RK4 steps (one Jacobian of F over [x; u]: nx + 1 dual directions, about the cost of reverse mode at this size, with no tape).

Objective, constraints and barriers

\[\min_{u_1,\dots,u_H}\ \sum_{k=1}^{H}\ell(x_{k+1},u_k)+\phi(x_{H+1}),\qquad 0\le u_k\le u_{max}\]
\[\ell=q_B\,\tfrac{N+C}{K}+q_R\,\tfrac{C}{K}+q_T\Big(\tfrac{T}{B_{tox}}\Big)^2+r_u\Big(\tfrac{u}{u_{max}}\Big)^2+b_{\mu_C}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{N+C}{B_{cancer}}\Big)+b_{\mu_T}\!\Big(1-\tfrac{T}{B_{tox}}\Big)\]
\[b_\mu(z)=\begin{cases}-\mu\log z, & z\ge\delta\\ \mu\left[\tfrac12\left(\tfrac{z-2\delta}{\delta}\right)^2-\tfrac12-\log\delta\right], & z<\delta\end{cases}\qquad \phi=5.0\left[q_B\,\tfrac{N+C}{K}+q_R\,\tfrac{C}{K}+q_T\Big(\tfrac{T}{B_{tox}}\Big)^2\right]\]

Goal. Keep the total tumour burden low and hold resistant cells back. The burden term \(q_B(N+C)/K\) is linear: every cell costs the same however large the tumour is, so the controller still treats a small culture when the drug removes cells. The resistance premium \(q_R\,C/K\) makes a resistant cell cost more than a sensitive one (with \(q_R=q_B\) it counts as two cells), because further cisplatin cannot remove it. Sensitive cells are never rewarded: a schedule that leaves more cells in total is worse unless it buys a larger drop in resistant cells. The hard limit on the burden is the cancer barrier at B_cancer. Precedents: tumour burden as a linear cost (Schättler & Ledzewicz 2015) and in-vitro chemotherapy optimised to avoid resistant growth (Carrère 2017).

States are normalised (by K and B_tox) so that numbers of cells do not swamp the toxicity terms. The cancer barrier grows as N + C nears B_cancer and favours treating; the toxicity barrier grows as T nears B_tox and favours stopping; the schedule is the balance of the two, not a hard-coded rule. The relaxed barrier (Feller & Ebenbauer 2017) is the exact log barrier inside the feasible set and a steep quadratic below z = δ, so a start where the bound cannot be met still gives a finite, informative cost; violations are counted in the tables, never hidden. Defaults: q_B = q_R = q_T = 1, r_u = 0.1, μ = 0.05, δ = 0.02; the weights (including q_R = 0, burden only, and q_R = 3) are swept below.

Controller: sequential (SQP) MPC. At each decision time: (1) simulate the nonlinear model over the horizon with the current plan; (2) linearise F along it (A_k, B_k by automatic differentiation); (3) gradient by the adjoint recursion \(\mu_k=\nabla\ell_k+A_k^\top\mu_{k+1}\) (vector–Jacobian products); (4) Gauss–Newton Hessian from the forward sensitivities \(S_{k+1}=A_kS_k+B_ke_k^\top\); (5) solve the box-constrained quadratic subproblem; (6) line search on the true nonlinear cost; repeat to convergence; apply the first dose; re-plan at the next media change. The local subproblem has the LQR structure \(\delta x_{k+1}=A_k\delta x_k+B_k\delta u_k\), but it is re-linearised every iteration, bounded, and has non-quadratic barriers, so this is successive-linearisation MPC, not a fixed LQR.

Two modes. Data-supported: doses restricted to 0, 0.62, 1.0 and 1.47 µM (the measured doses); the finite-horizon problem is then solved exactly by searching every dose sequence (prefix sharing and pruning), 4⁷ sequences over 14 days. Demonstration: continuous doses up to 3.0 µM with SQP; above 1.47 µM the linear kill is extrapolated and the result is not supported by data.

If the schedule barely changes across plausible weights, the recommendation does not hinge on an arbitrary weight choice.

Tests of two model assumptions

Drug washout. The drug-effect compartments assume the effect decays after the drug is removed at the same rate it built up. The intermittent experiments (1 µM on then off, or off then on, at day 7) test this: a separate washout rate is not supported (A2780: ΔBIC +2.06; A2780cis: ΔBIC +2.94; a negative value beyond −3 would be needed). The equal-rate model is kept, and its no-refit predictions of those experiments are on the Validation page.

Low resource with media changes. Media are changed every 2 days, so a resource state R that is depleted between changes and reset at each change was compared with the carried constant effect (both a simple exponential depletion and a cell-driven depletion \(dR/dt=-\lambda X R/1000\)). Neither passes the 3-BIC rule, so the constant low-resource effect is kept.

What the data do not identify

  • The toxicity limit is inactive at the default settings. Since \(dT/dt=(1-T)\big[a_H(1-e^{-B})-r_H\,T\big]\), T can never exceed \(a_H/r_H\) = 0.1, below the bound B_tox = 0.25, whatever the dose. The toxicity term in the cost still discourages dosing, but the hard limit only matters in the sweep with slower host recovery (r_H = 0.2 /day, bound \(a_H/r_H\) = 0.5). The schedules are therefore not toxicity-constrained in the main runs.
  • How each scenario is validated. Scenario A (the Stage 3a coculture fit) passes leave-one-ratio-out validation, but it was fitted to the treated cocultures, so there is no held-out test of its drug response in coculture. Scenario B is the construction that was tested on held-out data: crowding fitted to the untreated cocultures only, then the treated cocultures predicted with no refit (Validation).
  • Dose response outside 0.62–1.47 µM. The monocultures were treated at three doses and the cocultures at 1 µM only. The carried kill is linear in the drug effect, so it never saturates: it cannot show a maximum kill at high doses. Continuous-dose control above 1.47 µM (demonstration mode) extrapolates the linear kill.
  • Resistant kill in coculture. The cocultures were treated at one dose only, so how strongly cisplatin kills A2780cis in coculture rests on the monoculture fit: scenario A uses the coculture refit (bounded below by half the monoculture value, so it is never switched off) and scenario B the monoculture drug response for both lines; every result is given for both.
  • Toxicity. Nothing in these in-vitro data measures host toxicity. Its structure and values come from de Pillis & Radunskaya (2001); the mapping from medium concentration to systemic burden (s_B) and B_cancer are assumptions. Treat toxicity-limited results as illustrative.
  • Beyond 14 days. The 28-day runs extrapolate every stage beyond the data.
  • Low resource + drug. Stage 4 is fitted to a single 1:1 culture at one dose; its low-resource strengths ε_N, ε_C are identified only for that condition. Its first observation is one day after exposure started; the hidden drug and stress states at that point are simulated once from the candidates' starting values and held fixed during fitting (a small approximation).
  • Parameter uncertainty propagation. The bootstrap combines replicates across stages, but each stage was bootstrapped with earlier-stage values held fixed, so the bands are approximate.

Experiments that would most improve control

  1. Coculture dose series (e.g. 0.25, 0.5, 2 and 4 µM at 50:50, 14 days): identifies the coculture dose response and whether A2780cis is killed in coculture (scenario A vs B), the largest structural uncertainty for control, and lets the shape of the dose response (linear or saturating) be estimated in the condition that is being controlled.
  2. Coculture washout / intermittent schedules (1 µM on for 2 or 4 days then off, repeated): tests the drug-effect decay and the regrowth after removal in the controlled system; the monoculture intermittent data only test it for single lines.
  3. Replicate-level counts over time. The sensitivity analysis ranks sensitive growth r_N, coculture death d_N and the crowding coefficients as the main drivers of the controlled outcome; more replicates of the untreated and treated cocultures narrow exactly those.
  4. Low resource + cisplatin at a second dose. Stage 4 has one condition; a second dose separates the low-resource strengths from a drug × low-resource interaction.
  5. A toxicity readout (e.g. viability of a non-cancer line such as fibroblasts under the same schedules) would replace the literature toxicity proxy.
