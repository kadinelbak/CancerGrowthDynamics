Objective, soft penalties and barriers

\[\min_{u_1,\dots,u_H}\ \sum_{k=1}^{H}\ell(x_{k+1},u_k)+\phi(x_{H+1}),\qquad 0\le u_k\le u_{max}\]

Goal: time to progression (user, 2026-10-07). Progression is N + C at or above B_prog = 0.5 K. The step σ (width s = 0.01 K) is ≈ 0 below B_prog and ≈ 1 above it, so with q_P = 10.0 the summed stage cost counts the intervals spent progressed: the controller holds the culture under B_prog as long as it can, treating hard when it nears the threshold and holding off when it does not, the quantity adaptive therapy maximises (Zhang et al. 2017; Gatenby et al. 2009). At the end of the look-ahead \(q_{RT}\,C/K\) (q_RT = 10.0) prices the resistant cells left behind: they drive the next progression, which no dose can reverse. Progression is either line over its own threshold (rule "lines", user 2026-11-03): resistant cells at 0.5 K or sensitive cells at 0.85 × their untreated plateau. Everything else forms a second tier at 0.1 × its weight (user, 2026-11-12): the total burden (q_B = 1.0), the resistant cells (q_R = 1.0), toxicity, dose and the inside of the host barriers; crossing a host limit keeps the full penalty. The objective without progression (q_P = q_RT = 0) and the 2026-11-01 weights are rows of the weight sweep. Time to progression is the start of the first spell above B_prog that lasts at least 10.0 days (or to the end of the run): a brief excursion brought back under the threshold does not count, and one dip after a long spell above does not restart the clock.

Time to progression first, and runs stop once the answer is known (user, 2026-11-01). The progression term charges the time spent at or above B_prog, averaged over 8 pieces of each interval, and every other term (burden, resistant cells, dose, toxicity, and in the fasting runs host weight and fasting) is multiplied by 0.1, so it only breaks ties between plans that progress at the same time. The look-ahead is fixed (no end-of-run cost), so the final count is not a target. The comparators always run the whole run. An MPC run stops computing once progression is confirmed at 6 successive decisions (10 days; the controller keeps re-planning through them to bring the culture back), or once it has gone 10.0 days past the latest comparator's progression without progressing; its time to progression is read up to that day (the CSV column control_stopped_day) and the rest of the run follows its last plan, not re-optimised. The run length is therefore a cap.

Progression rule (user, 2026-11-03): the culture counts as progressed when the resistant line reaches 0.5 K or the sensitive line reaches 0.85 × its untreated plateau (not N + C ≥ 0.5 K as before). The cost uses the smooth OR \(1-(1-\sigma_N)(1-\sigma_C)\) of the two steps.

Previous goal (2026-10-02 to 2026-10-07). Keep the total tumour burden low and hold resistant cells back. The burden term \(q_B(N+C)/K\) is linear: every cell costs the same however large the tumour is, so the controller still treats a small culture when the drug removes cells. The resistance premium \(q_R\,C/K\) makes a resistant cell cost more than a sensitive one (with \(q_R=q_B\) it counts as two cells), because further cisplatin cannot remove it. Sensitive cells are never rewarded: a schedule that leaves more cells in total is worse unless it buys a larger drop in resistant cells. On top of the linear burden there is a soft burden penalty (a relaxed barrier above B_cancer = 0.5 K), not a hard cap. MPC therefore minimises this weighted cost (burden, resistance premium, toxicity, dose and the soft penalties), not the burden alone, so a fixed policy can end with fewer cells; each results section compares MPC with the best fixed policy on every metric and plots all policies on final cells against dose. Precedents: tumour burden as a linear cost (Schättler & Ledzewicz 2015) and in-vitro chemotherapy optimised to avoid resistant growth (Carrère 2017).

States are normalised (by K and E_lim) so that numbers of cells do not swamp the toxicity terms. The burden penalty grows as N + C nears B_cancer and favours treating; the toxicity barrier grows as T nears B_tox and favours stopping; the schedule is the balance of the two, not a hard-coded rule. The relaxed barrier (Feller & Ebenbauer 2017) is the exact log barrier inside the limit and a steep quadratic below z = δ, so a state beyond the limit still gives a finite, informative cost; exceedances are counted in the tables (whether and for how many days), never hidden. Defaults: q_P = 10.0, q_RT = 10.0, q_B = 1.0, q_R = 1.0, q_T = 1.0, r_u = 0.1, μ = 0.05, δ = 0.02, terminal factor 5.0; the weights (including the previous objective, no terminal resistant cost and a 3× one) are swept below.

Why a penalty and not a hard cap. In normal medium the culture grows towards its capacity K under the measured doses: 29 of 48 normal-medium runs (3 ratios × 2 scenarios × 8 policies) pass B_cancer = 0.5 K at some time, the data-supported MPC in 3 of 6 (for 3.3 days, median). As a hard state constraint, every schedule that cannot hold the culture below B_cancer would be infeasible and the optimiser would have no cost to rank them by; as a penalty, they are still ranked, and the exceedance is reported. A relaxed log barrier is, by construction, a soft-constraint (penalty) formulation (Feller & Ebenbauer 2017), and in cancer optimal control a tumour-burden limit is usually a penalised state constraint or a progression threshold rather than a hard bound (e.g. adaptive-therapy thresholds set relative to the burden, Zhang et al. 2017). B_cancer is therefore reported as the level where a soft penalty starts to dominate. Controller: sequential (SQP) MPC. At each decision time: (1) simulate the nonlinear model over the horizon with the current plan; (2) linearise F along it (A_k, B_k by automatic differentiation); (3) gradient by the adjoint recursion \(\mu_k=\nabla\ell_k+A_k^\top\mu_{k+1}\) (vector–Jacobian products); (4) Gauss–Newton Hessian from the forward sensitivities \(S_{k+1}=A_kS_k+B_ke_k^\top\); (5) solve the box-constrained quadratic subproblem; (6) line search on the true nonlinear cost; repeat to convergence; apply the first dose; re-plan at the next media change. The local subproblem has the LQR structure \(\delta x_{k+1}=A_k\delta x_k+B_k\delta u_k\), but it is re-linearised every iteration, bounded, and has non-quadratic barriers, so this is successive-linearisation MPC, not a fixed LQR.

Two modes. Data-supported: doses restricted to 0, 0.62, 1.0 and 1.47 µM (the measured doses); the finite-horizon problem is then solved exactly by searching every dose sequence (prefix sharing, a warm-start bound and pruning) at each of the 7 media changes. The 28-day look-ahead is split into 6 blocks of 1, 1, 2, 2, 4, 4 intervals with one dose each (move blocking, Cagienard et al. 2007), so 4096 sequences are searched; the first block is a single interval, so the dose actually applied is decided at full resolution. Demonstration: continuous doses up to 3.0 µM with SQP; above 1.47 µM the linear kill is extrapolated

Fixed look-ahead. Every decision, including the last ones, plans 28 days ahead (14 before 2026-10-07; both are in the horizon sweep); near the end of the run the plan simply runs past day 14 (the model is simulated beyond it). The schedule shown is therefore the first 7 decisions of a continuing policy, read off at day 14, not a plan that stops caring about what happens after its last day (a shrinking horizon stops dosing near the end, because later regrowth no longer counts).

Sensitivity of the controller

Tie-break scale (scenario A, 50:50). The second tier is scaled by tiebreak_scale = 0.1 (default). Tests of two model assumptions

Drug washout. The drug-effect compartments assume the effect decays after the drug is removed at the same rate it built up. The intermittent experiments (1 µM on then off, or off then on, at day 7) test this: a separate washout rate is not supported (A2780: ΔBIC +2.93; A2780cis: ΔBIC +3.21; a negative value beyond −2 would be needed). The equal-rate model is kept, and its no-refit predictions of those experiments are on the Validation page.

What the data do not identify

  • Kidney limit. A constant dose u holds E at \(E_{max}\gamma u/(EC_{50}+\gamma u)\) = 1.563 per µM-equivalent at 1 µM, against E_lim = 5.479; the limit binds only near 3.506 µM of steady exposure (beyond the measured doses) unless γ is larger (swept ×3, ×10).
  • How each scenario is validated. Scenario A (the Stage 3a coculture fit, the main result) reproduces the treated cocultures, since it was fitted to them; leave-one-ratio-out validation tests it on held-out ratios, and the plant-vs-data table above checks the controller's plant against the measured day-14 counts. Scenario B was built without the treated cocultures (Ce0 crowding, monoculture kills) and predicts them poorly: Scenario B is a what-if: the monoculture drug response applied in coculture. The treated cocultures contradict it: at a constant 1 µM it predicts a day-14 total of 592 cells at 50:50 against 3310 measured (scenario A, the Stage 3a coculture fit: 3398). (Validation).
  • Dose response outside 0.62–1.47 µM. The monocultures were treated at three doses and the cocultures at 1 µM only. The carried kill is linear in the drug effect, so it never saturates: it cannot show a maximum kill at high doses. Continuous-dose control above 1.47 µM (demonstration mode) extrapolates the linear kill.
  • Resistant kill in coculture. The coculture kill factors (φ) were measured at 1 µM only, the one dose the cocultures received, and are applied unchanged at every dose, so scenario A at 0.62 and 1.47 µM extrapolates them; scenario B is the bound for a resistant kill as in monoculture. The coculture fit finds no detectable resistant kill at 1 µM (φ_C ≈ 0), so in scenario A the controller cannot kill resistant cells at any dose; scenario B shows what the controller would do if the resistant cells kept their monoculture kill. Both are reported; A is the headline (user, 2026-11-12; B was the headline 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-12).
  • Toxicity. Nothing in these in-vitro data measures host toxicity. The kidney model and its values come from patients (Xie et al. 2020); the medium concentration is used as a stand-in for plasma level, and the scale γ and the limit E_lim are calibrated to the clinical schedule (an assumption, swept ×1, ×3, ×10). Treat toxicity-limited results as illustrative. B_cancer is an assumption too.
  • Beyond 14 days. The 28-day runs extrapolate every stage beyond the data.
  • Low resource + drug. Stage 4 is fitted to a single 1:1 culture at one dose; its low-resource strengths ε_N, ε_C are identified only for that condition. Its first observation is one day after exposure started; the hidden drug and stress states at that point are simulated once from the candidates' starting values and held fixed during fitting (a small approximation).
  • Parameter uncertainty propagation. The bootstrap combines replicates across stages, but each stage was bootstrapped with earlier-stage values held fixed, so the bands are approximate. Scenario B's crowding (fitted to the untreated cocultures) has no bootstrap of its own: its draws borrow the Stage 3a bootstrap ratios (replicate ÷ estimate) of the same parameters.
  • Measurement and state estimation. The closed-loop runs assume each media change gives a count of N and C with 10% noise and that the hidden drug-effect and toxicity states follow the planner's own model; a real loop needs the two lines counted separately at each media change, as the coculture experiments did.

Experiments that would most improve control

  1. Coculture dose series (e.g. 0.25, 0.5, 2 and 4 µM at 50:50, 14 days): identifies the coculture dose response and whether A2780cis is killed in coculture (scenario A vs B), the largest structural uncertainty for control, and lets the shape of the dose response (linear or saturating) be estimated in the condition that is being controlled.
  2. Coculture washout / intermittent schedules (1 µM on for 2 or 4 days then off, repeated): tests the drug-effect decay and the regrowth after removal in the controlled system; the monoculture intermittent data only test it for single lines.
  3. Replicate-level counts over time. The sensitivity analysis ranks sensitive growth r_N, coculture death d_N and the crowding coefficients as the main drivers of the controlled outcome; more replicates of the untreated and treated cocultures narrow exactly those.
  4. Low resource + cisplatin at a second dose. Stage 4 has one condition; a second dose separates the low-resource strengths from a drug × low-resource interaction.
  5. A toxicity readout (e.g. viability of a non-cancer or kidney-derived line such as HK-2 under the same schedules) would anchor the literature toxicity model to these exposures.
