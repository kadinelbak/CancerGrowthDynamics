Optimal control over 30 days: cisplatin and fasting

Generated 2026-10-03 12:38 by run_control_fasting.jl and control_fasting_report.jl. Model-predictive control now chooses two things at every media change (every 2 days) for 30 days: the cisplatin dose and whether the next 2 days are run in low resource ("fasting", 0.5% FBS). The goal is the same as in the 14-day report: a low total tumour burden with resistant cells held back (a resistant cell counts as two). Fasting has a host cost (body weight, with the cancer-cachexia threshold as a limit) and a host benefit (protection of healthy tissue from the drug), both taken from the fasting-and-chemotherapy literature. The look-ahead is 14 days, re-planned every 2 days. Scenarios A and B are as in the 14-day report.

Key findings (50:50 start)

  • Scenario A. MPC with fasting ends at 1197 cells (0 resistant) vs 2300 (150) for MPC without fasting and 4085 (3279) for constant 1 µM; it fasts on days 0–2, 26–28 (4 days in total), gives 38.2 µM·d of cisplatin, and keeps weight ≥ 0.975 and toxicity ≤ 0.077.
  • Scenario B. MPC with fasting ends at 143 cells (0 resistant) vs 644 (245) for MPC without fasting and 3834 (3833) for constant 1 µM; it fasts on days 0–2 (2 days in total), gives 34.6 µM·d of cisplatin, and keeps weight ≥ 0.976 and toxicity ≤ 0.077.

These results rest on how slowly starvation damage in A2780cis fades after refeeding, which was never measured (see How much rests on recovery after refeeding): if it fades in about a day and a half (k_off = 2/day), the scenario-A MPC plan ends with 1723 resistant cells instead of 0, and re-optimising gives 132 with 4 fasting days.

Read first. Days 14–30 are beyond the data (shaded on every plot). The tumour-side effect of fasting is the in-vitro serum-starvation effect at full strength, with no in-vivo attenuation yet, so fasting's benefit is an upper bound. The host states are literature-based proxies, not measurements. These are model simulations; no schedule here has been tested.

The model the controller uses

Two controls, each held constant over a 2-day interval between media changes: the cisplatin concentration \(u\) (µM) and fasting \(v\) (1 = the interval is run in 0.5% FBS, 0 = fed, 10% FBS). Fasting enters the cells through the fitted low-resource level \(u_R = 0.9547738693467337\,v\), and the host through the weight and protection states. When \(v = 0\) the model is exactly the 14-day control model; with \(u = 1\) µM and \(v = 1\) it is exactly the fitted Stage 4 model (both checked below).

How much rests on recovery after refeeding

In the fitted low-resource model (Stage 2a), starvation stress in A2780cis builds up through a three-compartment chain at rate \(k_C^R = 0.1113\)/day, and after refeeding it fades at the same slow rate (about 27 days). One 48-h fast therefore keeps killing resistant cells for weeks, which is why the controller needs so little fasting. No experiment measured recovery after refeeding: every low-resource culture stayed in 0.5% FBS for all 14 days, and the stress-death coefficient is poorly identified (flat profile likelihood). Here the stress is allowed to fade faster once fed, \(dS_{C,1}/dt = k\,(\varepsilon_C u_R - S_{C,1})\) with \(k = k_C^R\,v + k_{off}(1-v)\), and the same schedules are replayed and re-optimised.

Experiment that would settle it: A2780cis (and a 1:1 coculture) in 0.5% FBS for 48 h, then back to 10% FBS, counted daily for 10–14 days.

Tests

  • Fed (v = 0) reproduces the 14-day control model exactly; u = 1 µM with v = 1 reproduces the fitted Stage 4 model.
  • The fast, type-stable right-hand side used by the controller equals the zoo's generic right-hand side to 10⁻¹².
  • Fixed-step RK4 matches an adaptive Tsit5 solve; automatic-differentiation Jacobians A = ∂F/∂x and B = ∂F/∂(u, v) match central finite differences; the adjoint gradient matches finite differences of the cost.
  • The exact search equals brute force with the fasting rules, and a warm start never changes its answer; SQP keeps both controls in bounds and never raises the cost.
  • Weight, protection and toxicity follow their equations; with p_H = 0 fasting gives no protection.

What the data do not support

  • No in-vivo attenuation yet. The tumour-side effect of fasting is the in-vitro effect of 0.5% FBS at full strength. A patient's fast changes serum glucose, insulin and IGF-1 far less than a 20-fold serum cut, so the benefit shown here is an upper bound. Scaling it down is the planned next step.
  • Recovery after refeeding of starvation damage was never measured, and the result depends on it strongly (section above).
  • Days 14–30 extrapolate every stage beyond the data.
  • Fasting without drug is predicted less accurately than by the dedicated Stage 3b fit (checks above), and the drug × low-resource interaction was measured at one dose only (1 µM).
  • Host states are literature-informed proxies: nothing in these experiments measured toxicity, weight or protection. The weight rates come from a case series, the cachexia threshold from a consensus definition, and the protection strength is an assumption (the randomized DIRECT trial found less DNA damage in T cells but no difference in toxicity grades, so p_H = 0 is in the sweep).
  • Resistant kill in coculture (scenario A vs B) remains the largest structural uncertainty, as in the 14-day report.
