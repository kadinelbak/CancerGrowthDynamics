Neural vs mechanistic — held-out scorecard

How to read this

The neural models (boosted trees, MLP, a UDE with a neural kill term, a pure neural ODE) are a competing method. Each row takes the best neural and the best non-neural model on one held-out test. The non-neural side holds the mechanistic ODEs, including the no-drug model, and for next-day prediction also the persistence and ridge baselines. difference = mechanistic minus neural error (positive = neural better). Rule for ties: a difference is a tie when it is below 0.02 log units or below the seed-to-seed SD of the neural score (or, for Stage 6, when the paired-bootstrap 95% CI includes 0). Every model is autonomous (no clock-time inputs or terms); the next-day regression features carry no clock time either.

What these data can and cannot say

These tests cover 4 constant doses (0–1.47 µM), two on/off schedules at 1 µM, two seeding densities and one held-out treated co-culture condition. They do not test schedules with more than one switch, doses outside 0–1.47 µM, other cell lines, or data sets much larger than these; no statement here should be read as covering those. The three-way comparison (Stage 6) is the fairest single test: same held-out points, same training data, same starting-value rule.
